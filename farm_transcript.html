YouSo with the additional piglets, are there any welfare issues likely?
FMIS

Likely, yes. The main risk isn't square-metres — it's that the data shows one undifferentiated pig group with no farrowing sub-pens, and two sows due to farrow into that shared space within ~4 months.

  • Crushing and savaging — group farrowing without protected creeps drives pre-weaning death; expect 15–25%+ litter mortality rather than ~10–15%.
  • Chilling — newborns need a 30–34 °C micro-climate; ambient is ~15 °C and cooling, with no creep provision recorded.
  • Overcrowding as litters grow — the sow group already sits at ~45–55 pigs/ha; piglets at 25–30 kg by ten weeks push bedding and fouling load hard.
  • Neonatal disease — higher density plus young stock builds up scour pathogens, joint ill, greasy pig disease.
  • Sow condition — several females already light (Blush 200 kg, Clifford 203 kg); group feeding lets dominant sows push lactating sows off feed.

From the current data: Goliath's collar is down — a sow with no location/activity/heart-rate telemetry as farrowing nears. And three mature boars share a pen next to in-heat sows (fighting risk).

Caveat: if farrowing arcs or a farrowing house exist but simply aren't recorded as entities, the first three points largely go away. Confirm on the ground before the first sow is due.
