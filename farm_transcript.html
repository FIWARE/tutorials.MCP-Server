YouSo with the additional piglets, are there any welfare issues likely?
FMIS

Yes — layering projected piglets onto the current setup raises several real risks, mostly stemming from things the data shows are missing rather than present:

  • No farrowing infrastructure: no building, ark, or shelter is recorded for any pig — everything is open paddock. Newborn piglets can't thermoregulate and are highly vulnerable to chilling and rain exposure without a farrowing hut.
  • Crushing risk: without individual farrowing pens, sows farrowing in a shared open area with 35 other adults around is the single biggest driver of piglet mortality in pig farming (commonly 10-20%+ pre-weaning loss when this isn't managed).
  • Boar-piglet aggression: 4 boars are mixed in with the sows. Boars housed alongside lactating sows and new litters is a known cause of piglet injury/death — they're normally separated out well before farrowing.
  • Space already tight: the paddock was already borderline (~162m²/pig vs. the ~250m²/pig outdoor guideline) for 40 adults. Adding 350-950 piglets to that same footprint, even temporarily, would push density well past workable limits unless the area is expanded or sows are rotated into dedicated farrowing paddocks.

Net: on the data as recorded, welfare issues look likely, not just possible — the farm has no visible provision for farrowing separation, shelter, or expanded space to absorb a litter surge.

YouWhat would you recommend to fix these gaps?
FMIS

Practical fixes, roughly in priority order:

  1. Install farrowing arks/huts — one per sow due to farrow, out on the paddock or in a dedicated area. Gives piglets a draft-free, insulated space to bed down away from the herd, and cuts chilling deaths.
  2. Separate boars from the farrowing area — pull the 4 boars out into their own paddock once sows are due, and keep them out until piglets are weaned. Removes the single biggest piglet-injury risk.
  3. Stagger farrowing rather than batch it — group sows into 3-4 breeding batches instead of all 36 farrowing at once. Spreads the piglet surge over time so you're never managing hundreds of newborns in one paddock simultaneously.
  4. Expand or rotate paddock space during farrowing — give farrowing sows a separate, lower-density paddock (aim for the ~250m²/pig outdoor guideline) rather than adding piglets to the existing crowded 162m²/pig space.
  5. Track farrowing and mortality once it starts — none of this is in the broker yet (no birthdate, litter, or mortality attributes on the pig records). Once sows start farrowing, that data should get recorded so you can actually see if crushing/chilling losses are happening rather than estimating blind.
  6. Re-check the boar ratio at scale — 1:9 is fine for the current 36 sows, but if the herd grows, keep it under ~1:15-20 to avoid over-competition among boars.
